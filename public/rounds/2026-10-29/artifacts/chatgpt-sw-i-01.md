# Decision note — Apple Watch Series 10 vs Ultra 2

**Shopping scenario:** pool swimming plus recreational scuba diving to 30 m (approximately 98 ft).

**Decision:** Apple Watch Ultra 2 is the only one of these two models specified by Apple for recreational scuba at 30 m. Both are suitable for pool swimming. Apple Watch Series 10 is not specified for scuba diving.

| Distinction | Apple Watch Series 10 | Apple Watch Ultra 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Water-resistance rating | 50 m under ISO 22810:2010 | 100 m under ISO 22810:2010 |
| Pool swimming | Supported | Supported |
| Snorkeling / shallow underwater use | Up to 6 m | Supported, with depth sensing up to 40 m |
| Depth app limit | 6 m (20 ft) | 40 m (130 ft) |
| Recreational scuba | Not supported | Supported to 40 m (130 ft), with compatible third-party dive-computer app |
| Diving-related standard | No EN13319 compliance specified | EN13319 compliant |
| Proposed scuba at 30 m | Not suitable | Within Apple's stated 40 m limit |

**Interpretation:** The 50 m and 100 m resistance ratings are not permissible scuba depths. Activity-specific limits govern. Apple's built-in Depth app is *not* a dive computer and does not provide decompression-stop or gas analysis functions. A compatible third-party app is needed for dive-computer features on Ultra 2.

**Question before purchase:** Do you need the Ultra 2 to function as your dive computer at 30 m, and if so, have you confirmed that a compatible third-party dive app supports the planned dives?

**Unresolved conditions:** The intended dive-computer app and its compatibility, suitable water-resistant band, and the condition of the particular watch are not confirmed. Water resistance can diminish over time; Apple notes it cannot be rechecked or resealed for water resistance. This is a specification comparison, not diving training or an individualized safety certification.
